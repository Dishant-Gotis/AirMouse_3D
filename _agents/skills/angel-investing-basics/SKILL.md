# Angel Investing Basics

## What is Angel Investing?

**Definition**: Individual investors providing capital to early-stage companies (typically $10k-$250k per company)

**Characteristics**:
- **Stage**: Pre-seed to Series A (companies 0-24 months old)
- **Check size**: $10k-$500k (depends on experience, net worth)
- **Holding period**: 5-10 years (venture capital exits)
- **Risk**: 70% of deals fail, returns concentrated in winners
- **Target return**: 10-30x+ on winners (offset failures with home runs)

## Angel vs VC

| Factor | Angel | VC |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Check size | $10k-250k | $500k-5M+ |
| Stage | Pre-seed, Seed | Series A, B, C |
| Companies in portfolio | 20-40+ | 30-50+ |
| Due diligence | Light | Extensive |
| Board seat | Sometimes | Usually |
| Hands-on help | Variable | Yes (mentor, network, expertise) |
| Liquidity | 5-10 years | 5-10 years |

## Finding Deals

### Deal sources (by quality)

**Personal network** (Best deals):
- Friend/family introducing you to founder
- Founder you know launching new company
- Colleague leaving to start company
- **Advantage**: Founder quality you can assess, trust established

**Angel groups/syndicates**:
- **AngelList**: Syndicate model (lead investor + followers)
- **SeedInvest**: Curated deal flow, equity crowdfunding
- **Forge**: Secondary market (buy/sell angel stakes)
- **Advantage**: Curated deals, due diligence shared, small check size ($1k-$50k)

**Events/conferences**:
- Startup pitch events (local accelerators, pitch competitions)
- Industry conferences (founders pitch at booths)
- University entrepreneur days
- **Advantage**: Meet founders in person, see pitches

**Online platforms**:
- **Linkedin**: Search "raising seed round" or "looking for angel investors"
- **Twitter**: Founders in your space discussing fundraising
- **Indie Hackers**: Side project founders
- **Advantage**: Large funnel, geographic flexibility

**Venture firms**:
- Partner at VC firm invites you to invest alongside them (co-invest)
- **Advantage**: Professional due diligence, board governance

## Deal Evaluation

### Initial screening (5-minute filter)

**Red flags** (pass immediately):
- ❌ Founders with no relevant experience
- ❌ Market too small (< $1B TAM) or too saturated
- ❌ Vague problem statement ("help people save money")
- ❌ Product that doesn't exist yet (too early risk)
- ❌ Poor communication (can't articulate vision)

**Green lights** (dig deeper):
- ✓ Strong founding team (relevant experience, complementary skills)
- ✓ Clear problem (specific pain point, quantified)
- ✓ Traction (users, revenue, or strong MVP)
- ✓ Large market (total addressable market > $1B)
- ✓ Defensible advantage (technology, network effects, brand)

### Deep due diligence

**Founder assessment**:
- Passion for problem (why them specifically?)
- Track record (previous successes/failures)
- Team composition (technical, sales, product)
- Hunger/determination (will do whatever it takes)

**Product assessment**:
- Does it solve the problem? (Not assumed, validated with users)
- Is it better than alternatives? (3-5x improvement required)
- Can it scale? (Unit economics work at 10x size?)
- Is there a competitive moat? (Why won't competitors copy?)

**Market assessment**:
- Is the market large? (TAM > $1B)
- Is it growing? (Ideally 30%+ YoY)
- Are customers willing to pay? (Proven via pilot sales)
- Are there any dynamics working against you? (Regulation, tech shifts)

**Financial assessment**:
- Unit economics (CAC, LTV, gross margin, payback period)
- Path to profitability (when break-even realistically?)
- Burn rate (months of runway, funding plan)
- Capital requirements (how much to reach next milestone?)

**Reference checks**:
- Talk to customers (are they actually using / happy?)
- Talk to previous team members (credibility check)
- Talk to investors in their previous company
- **Avoid**: Only references they provide (bias towards positive)

### Valuation assessment

**Methods**:
1. **Comparable companies**: What did similar startups raise at?
2. **Revenue multiple**: If revenue, what multiple is fair?
3. **VC method**: Work backward from exit value
4. **Feeling test**: Does the valuation feel right? (Gut check)

**Example**:
```
Comparable companies similar seed round:
- StartupA: $500k raised at $2.5M post-money valuation
- StartupB: $750k raised at $3M post-money valuation
- StartupC: $400k raised at $1.8M post-money valuation

This company's Series Seed:
- Raising: $500k
- Fair post-money: $2-3M
- Your 50k investment = 1.7-2.5% ownership (reasonable)
```

**Valuation red flags**:
- Too high valuation (sets up down round later = dilution)
- Too low (founder should be excited, not desperate)
- No revenue, but super high valuation (no traction to support)

## Investment Types

### SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
**Best for**: Seed stage, founder's first fundraise

**Terms**:
- No ownership yet (converts on future funding)
- Converts at discount (20-30% below Series A)
- Or converts at valuation cap ($X max valuation)
- Interest accrues (if Series A doesn't happen in 2 years)

**Pros**: Fast, cheap, simple
**Cons**: No investor rights, no board seat

### Convertible note
**Best for**: Seed stage, bridge round

**Terms**:
- Debt (bears interest, typically 4-8% annually)
- Converts to equity on next funding event
- Maturity date (becomes debt due if no Series A)
- Discount + interest rewards patient capital

**Pros**: Investor gets interest if exits early
**Cons**: More complex, matures as debt

### Preferred stock (Series Seed)
**Best for**: Seed stage with institutional interest, Series A

**Terms**:
- Direct equity ownership
- Liquidation preference (1x usually)
- Voting rights
- Protective provisions (investor veto on major decisions)

**Pros**: Clarity, investor protection
**Cons**: More complex documents, slower close ($10-20k legal fees)

## Portfolio Construction

### How many startups?

**Rule of thumb**: Invest in 20-50 companies to build a portfolio

**Why many?** Venture returns are power-law distributed:
```
Portfolio of 20 investments:
- ~14 fail completely (lose 100%)
- ~4 return 1-5x (get money back)
- ~1-2 return 10-50x (big winners)
```

Net result: 1-2 winners can carry the entire portfolio if sized right

### Check size strategy

**Equal-weight strategy**: $20k in each company
- Pros: Simple, diversified
- Cons: Miss concentrated bets, winners under-capitalized

**Power-law strategy**: $5k-$100k by conviction
- $100k in companies you love (top quartile)
- $25k in good opportunities
- $5k in interesting but riskier bets
- Pros: Capture upside in winners
- Cons: Need strong thesis/diligence

### Selection criteria

**Must-have**:
- [ ] Strong founder (relevant experience, track record)
- [ ] Clear problem (specific customer pain)
- [ ] Large market (TAM > $1B)

**Nice-to-have**:
- [ ] Traction (users, revenue)
- [ ] Differentiation (better than alternatives)
- [ ] Network effects (gets stronger with scale)

**Avoid**:
- [ ] Founder who won't listen to feedback
- [ ] Product before problem validation
- [ ] Tiny market (< $100M TAM)

## Rights & Follow-ons

### Investor rights (even for SAFE)

**Information rights**:
- Access to financial statements
- Board meeting updates
- Pro-rata participation in future rounds

**Pro-rata rights**:
- Right to invest in future rounds at same terms
- Maintain % ownership (avoid dilution)
- **Example**: Own 2% of seed, can buy 2% of Series A to stay at 2%

### Follow-on investing

**Best practice**: Reserve capital for follow-ons
- Participate in strong performers' Series A/B (concentrate winners)
- Let underperformers dilute (let them raise at lower valuation)

**Example**:
```
Initial angel: $20k for 1% of company (Series Seed)
Series A: $5M raised, your stake dilutes to 0.5%
Follow-on: Invest additional $50k to get back to 1%
Result: Concentrated bet in strong performer
```

## Exit scenarios

### Successful exit
Company sells or goes public:
- Early employees get liquidity (stock becomes cash)
- Investors get 5-50x returns (if invested at right stage)
- Founders become wealthy

**Timeline**: 5-10 years typical

### Down round
Company struggles, raises at lower valuation:
- Your shares diluted (anti-dilution provisions protect you slightly)
- But dilution is real (company worth less)

**Result**: Smaller return on that investment

### Zombie (stuck)
Company neither exits nor fails:
- Founder + small team keeps going
- Revenue covers expenses, but no growth
- No exit in sight
- **Result**: Capital stuck, zero return indefinitely

### Total loss
Company fails, shuts down:
- Investment becomes worthless
- **Frequency**: ~70% of seed investments

**This is why diversification matters**: 70% losses offset by 10-50x winners

## Managing your investments

### Board observer / updates
- Request quarterly updates (how is company doing?)
- Ask to attend board meetings (understand trajectory)
- Provide advice/introductions if helpful (mentor mindset)

### Secondary sales (liquidity)
**Forge, EquityZen**: Marketplaces to buy/sell pre-IPO shares

**Use case**:
- Need cash? Sell mature investment at premium
- Concentrated bet? Trim to average down
- Bad deal? Sell loss, reinvest elsewhere

### Tax implications

**Capital gains**:
- Short-term (< 1 year): Taxed as ordinary income
- Long-term (> 1 year): 15-20% tax rate (better)
- **Strategy**: Hold winners > 1 year if possible

**QSBS (Qualified Small Business Stock)**:
- If meet requirements (C-corp, active business, raised < $50M), can exclude capital gains up to $10M
- Requires 5-year hold
- **Big advantage**: If $50k investment becomes $500k, pay $0 tax on $450k gain

**Section 1244 (small business stock)**:
- Can deduct losses against ordinary income (not just capital gains)
- Max $50k per year ($100k married)
- Useful for failed investments

## Common angel mistakes

| Mistake | Impact | Fix |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Too few investments | Single failed bet wipes you out | Diversify across 20-40 companies |
| Check sizes too large | Capital tied up, can't follow-on | Start small ($5-25k), scale up |
| No due diligence | Invest in bad founders | Talk to customers, founders' references |
| Overweight conviction | Lose when big bet fails | No single check > 5% of portfolio |
| No follow-ons | Miss winners scaling | Reserve capital for follow-ons |
| Emotional investing | Invest in friend, not business | Separate relationship from investment |

## Getting started

1. **Build capital**: Accumulate $100k-$500k to invest
2. **Learn deals**: Attend pitch events, understand market
3. **Start angel groups**: Join other angel investors
4. **Make first 5 investments**: Small checks, learn from mistakes
5. **Build track record**: Learn what works, improve diligence
6. **Scale portfolio**: 20-40 companies across multiple years
