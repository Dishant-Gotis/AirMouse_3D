# Agency Operations: Run a Profitable Team

Most agencies are chaos. Without systems, you hire people and lose money. Here's how to run operations that scale.

## The Agency Operating System

### Principle 1: Everything Is a Project

```
PROJECT STRUCTURE:

Every client engagement = Project
Every project has:
  • Owner (point person)
  • Budget (revenue - estimated hours - software)
  • Timeline (start - end date)
  • Team assigned
  • Milestone checklist
  • Success metrics

EXAMPLE:

Project: ABC Corp Website Redesign
Owner: [Team Lead]
Client: ABC Corp
Budget: $15,000
Team: 1 Designer (60 hours), 1 Dev (80 hours), PM (20 hours)
Timeline: Jan 1 - Mar 31
Milestones:
  ☐ Discovery complete (Jan 15)
  ☐ Design approved (Feb 1)
  ☐ Dev complete (Feb 28)
  ☐ Testing (Mar 1-15)
  ☐ Launch (Mar 28)
Status: On track
Profitability: $15,000 revenue - $6,000 labor = $9,000 profit (60% margin)
```

### Principle 2: Billable vs. Overhead

Track every hour.

```
BILLABLE HOURS (Revenue-generating):
• Client work
• Content (if delivering to client)
• Fixing bugs (client requested)

OVERHEAD HOURS (Non-billable):
• Meetings (internal)
• Training
• Admin
• Recruitment
• Sales (if not project-based)
• Strategic planning

UTILIZATION TARGET:

Healthy agency: 70% billable, 30% overhead

If you're at:
80%+ billable: Burning out, no growth time
50% billable: Overhead bloated, need to cut costs
70% billable: Sweet spot

Track monthly per person:
Total hours worked: 160/month (40 hrs/week × 4 weeks)
Billable hours: 112 hours (70%)
Overhead: 48 hours (30%)
```

### Principle 3: Profitability Per Client

```
CLIENT PROFITABILITY SCORECARD:

Client: [Name]
Annual Revenue: $200,000
Annual Labor Cost: $120,000 (actual hours billed × rates)
  Designer: 1000 hours × $100 = $100,000
  PM: 100 hours × $50 = $5,000
  Dev: 200 hours × $75 = $15,000
Software/Tools Cost: $5,000
  (Webflow, design tools, hosting allocated)

Gross Profit: $200K - $120K - $5K = $75,000
Gross Margin: 37.5%

Is this healthy?

✓ 35%+ margin: Healthy (scale this client)
✓ 25-35% margin: Acceptable (optimize)
⚠ 15-25% margin: Watch (too many revisions? scope creep?)
✗ <15% margin: Red flag (losing money, need intervention)

INTERVENTION:
If margin too low:
→ Raise rates 20% (next year)
→ Tighten scope (cap revisions)
→ More efficient processes (reduce hours)
→ Fire client (if nothing improves)
```

## Project Management System

### The Weekly Project Health Check

Every Friday, 30 minutes per project:

```
PROJECT HEALTH STATUS:

Project: ABC Corp Website Redesign
Status: ON TRACK
Updated: Friday, Jan 26

METRICS:
Hours spent: 80 / 160 budgeted (50%)
Budget remaining: $7,500 (50%)
Timeline: Week 5 of 12 (42%)
Profit margin: 60% (on track)

NEXT MILESTONE: Design approval (Feb 1)
□ On track
☑ At risk (2 weeks to deliver, 80% complete, needs feedback)
□ Behind (more than 2 weeks late)

BLOCKER: Waiting on client brand colors (3 days late)
→ Owner: Sent 2 emails, following up Monday

TEAM UTILIZATION:
  Designer: 40 hrs / 60 budgeted (67% allocation, on track)
  Dev: 20 hrs / 80 budgeted (25%, will ramp month 2)
  PM: 5 hrs / 20 budgeted (25%, light touch this month)

RISKS:
  1. Client slow to approve (mitigated by setting 48-hour deadline)
  2. Scope creep on homepage design (2 small asks pending)

DECISIONS MADE:
  • Redesign hero section (didn't approve first concept)
  • Add email template (add-on, +$500 revenue)

NEXT WEEK:
  → Design review Thursday
  → Client approval Friday
  → Dev starts Monday (Feb 1)
```

This is your early warning system. Identify problems week 1, not week 10.

### Project Handoff Protocol

When a project ends:

```
FINAL CHECKLIST:

Delivery:
□ All files organized and handed off
□ Client has access (Webflow, figma, code repo, etc.)
□ Documentation complete

Close-out:
□ Final invoice sent
□ All hours logged
□ Time logged accurately for future cost estimation
□ Any unused budget noted (usually small refund)

Retrospective:
□ What went well? (1-2 things)
□ What could improve? (1-2 things)
□ Actual hours vs. estimated hours (for future pricing)
□ Client satisfaction score (1-10)
□ Would we take this client again? (YES/MAYBE/NO)

Example:
WELL: Client decision-making was fast, scope remained tight
IMPROVE: Testing phase took longer than planned (underestimated QA)
HOURS: Estimated 160 hrs, actual 175 hrs (90% accuracy)
SATISFACTION: 9/10 (very happy)
REPEAT: YES

File: Store in project folder for future reference
```

## Contractor Management

### Hiring the Right People

```
HIRE FOR:
1. Skill (can they do the work?)
2. Reliability (will they deliver on time?)
3. Communication (can they work with clients?)

LEAST IMPORTANT:
✗ Credentials (degree, certifications)
✗ Years of experience (5 years bad work > 1 year great work)
✗ Timezone (async work removes this constraint)
```

### Contractor Tier System

```
TIER 1: Junior Contractors ($30-50/hour)
Capabilities:
• Standard tasks (basic design, basic coding)
• Following clear instructions
• 90% supervise, 10% independent

Assignments:
• Defined scope (not ambiguous)
• Clear deadline
• Step-by-step process
• Weekly check-ins

Projects:
• Productized services (standard templates)
• Support tickets (predefined solutions)

TIER 2: Mid-Level Contractors ($50-100/hour)
Capabilities:
• Complex problems (custom design, integration)
• Some autonomy (figure things out)
• 50% supervise, 50% independent

Assignments:
• Broader scope ("Build checkout flow")
• Complex requirements
• Bi-weekly check-ins

Projects:
• Client projects (some variance)
• Features within larger projects

TIER 3: Senior Contractors ($100-200+/hour)
Capabilities:
• Full ownership (no supervision needed)
• Problem-solving
• Client-facing (if needed)
• 10% supervise, 90% independent

Assignments:
• Full project ownership
• Estimating their own work
• Monthly check-ins

Projects:
• Leading major initiatives
• Mentoring junior contractors
• Strategic work
```

### Contractor Playbook

Create one per contractor type.

```
TIER 1 CONTRACTOR PLAYBOOK — Junior Designer

SKILLS REQUIRED:
✓ Figma proficiency
✓ Responsive design principles
✓ Design systems understanding
✓ Clear communication

PROJECTS ASSIGNED:
• Landing pages (from template)
• Social media graphics
• Email templates
• Icon design

PROCESS:
1. I give you: Design brief + brand guidelines + Figma template
2. You: Create 3 design concepts
3. I: Review + feedback
4. You: Refine based on feedback
5. I: Final approval + QA

EXPECTED TIMELINE:
• Concepts: 3 days
• Feedback cycle: 2 days
• Final: 2 days
• Total: 7 days per project

TOOLS:
• Figma (for design)
• Slack (for communication)
• Google Drive (for files)

QUESTIONS?
• Ambiguous brief: Ask before starting
• Unsure of style: Reference designs are in Figma
• Stuck: Slack me (I usually respond same day)

REVIEW CRITERIA:
✓ Follows brand guidelines exactly
✓ Mobile responsive
✓ Typos? Zero
✓ Figma file organized
✓ On time

SUCCESS:
• 90%+ of concepts approved (if lower, brief-writing issue)
• On-time delivery (always)
• Client happiness (feedback is positive)
```

### Payment Structure

```
OPTION 1: Hourly ($X/hour)
Pros: Easy for variable work, low risk
Cons: No incentive to work efficiently

OPTION 2: Fixed per project ($X flat)
Pros: Incentive to work fast, margin protection
Cons: Hard to estimate accurately

OPTION 3: Retainer ($X per month for Y hours)
Pros: Predictable cost, commitment from contractor
Cons: Unused hours wasted if they don't use full allocation

RECOMMENDED:
Mix retainer + project bonuses

Example:
$2,000/month retainer for 80 hours + $50 bonus per project delivered early
= Predictable cost + incentive to move fast
```

## Operations Infrastructure

### Tooling

```
PROJECT MANAGEMENT:
→ Asana or Monday.com ($40-100/month for team)
  Tracks: Projects, timelines, dependencies, bottlenecks

TIME TRACKING:
→ Toggl or Clockify (free-$10/month)
  Tracks: Hours per project, billable vs overhead
  Rule: All contractors log time daily

COMMUNICATION:
→ Slack ($6.67/user/month)
  Channels: #projects, #operations, #random
  Rule: Decision emails get forwarded to Slack for visibility

INVOICING:
→ Freshbooks or Wave (free-$50/month)
  Tracks: Client invoices, payment status, revenue pipeline

FINANCIAL:
→ QuickBooks or Xero ($30-60/month)
  Tracks: Profit/loss, tax reporting, cash flow
  Rule: Update weekly with actual hours and costs
```

## The Scaling Trajectory

```
STAGE 1: Solo Freelancer ($60K/year)
You: All the work
Output: 5-8 projects/year
Profitability: You're the profit

STAGE 2: 1-2 Contractors ($150K/year)
You: Sales + project management
Contractors: 80% of delivery
Output: 15-20 projects/year
Profitability: $150K revenue - $50K contractor - $20K software = $80K profit

STAGE 3: 1 Full-time Employee ($300K/year)
You: Sales + operations
Employees: 100% of delivery
Output: 30-40 projects/year
Profitability: $300K revenue - $100K salary - $40K software - $20K other = $140K profit

STAGE 4: 2+ Full-time Employees ($600K+ /year)
You: CEO (sales, strategy, hiring)
Team: Project delivery + client relationships
Output: 60-80 projects/year
Profitability: $600K revenue - $200K salaries - $50K software = $350K profit
```

At Stage 3, you have a business. You can take a week off.

## Common Operations Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake 1: Not tracking time**
Result: You don't know which projects lose money. You underprice work.

**Mistake 2: Taking clients with low margins (<30%)**
Result: You're working hard, making no money. It compounds.

**Mistake 3: Not formalizing contractor relationships**
Result: Miscommunication, scope creep, quality issues.

**Mistake 4: Mixing billable and overhead hours**
Result: Can't see if you're actually profitable or just busy.

**Mistake 5: Scaling too fast**
Result: Quality drops, customers leave, profit collapses.

## Agency Operations Checklist

Weekly:
- [ ] Project status reviews (Friday, 30 min)
- [ ] Time tracking reviewed (accuracy)
- [ ] KPI dashboard updated
- [ ] Team communication

Monthly:
- [ ] Client profitability review (identify low-margin clients)
- [ ] Contractor feedback (satisfaction, issues)
- [ ] Project retrospectives (improve processes)
- [ ] Financial review (profit, burn rate, runway)

Quarterly:
- [ ] Raise rates 5-10% (market adjustment)
- [ ] Audit contractor tier (promote or move)
- [ ] Strategic hiring plan (next 3 contractors)
- [ ] Process improvements (which inefficiency to fix next)

The best agencies are boring. They have predictable profit, happy teams, and clear processes.
